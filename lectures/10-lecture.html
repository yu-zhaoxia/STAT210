2 Partial F-tests (General)

2.1 Full vs Reduced models (nested)

We test whether a subset of \(k\) coefficients are zero given the remaining predictors are in the model: \[ H_0:\ \beta_{j_1}=\cdots=\beta_{j_k}=0, \qquad H_a:\ \text{at least one of }\beta_{j_m}\neq 0. \]

2.2 Test statistic (SSE form)

Let \(\text{SSE}_F\) and \(\text{SSE}_R\) be the error sums of squares for the full and reduced models, respectively; let \(k\) be the number of tested coefficients, and \(df_F\) the residual df of the full model. Then \[ F=\frac{(\text{SSE}_R-\text{SSE}_F)/k}{\text{SSE}_F/df_F} \ \ \overset{H_0}\sim\ F_{k,\,df_F}. \]

2.3 Equivalent form (SSR)

\[ F=\frac{(\text{SSR}_F-\text{SSR}_R)/k}{\text{SSE}_F/df_F}. \]

3.3 Interpretation

The F-statistic is \[ F=\frac{(\text{SSE}_R-\text{SSE}_F)/2}{\text{SSE}_F/df_F}. \] A small \(p\)-value indicates income and/or X65plus add explanatory power beyond bachelor.

4 Extra Sums of Squares

4.1 Definition (subset given others)

\[ \text{SSR}(\text{subset}\mid\text{others}) = \text{SSR}(\text{all}) - \text{SSR}(\text{others}) = \text{SSE}(\text{others}) - \text{SSE}(\text{all}). \]

5 Decomposition and Order (Type I SS)

5.1 Two predictors (\(x_1,x_2\))

Decompose SST in two equivalent orders: \[\begin{align*} \text{SST} &= \text{SSR}(x_1) + \text{SSR}(x_2\mid x_1) + \text{SSE}(x_1,x_2) \\ &= \text{SSR}(x_2) + \text{SSR}(x_1\mid x_2) + \text{SSE}(x_1,x_2). \end{align*}\] Sequential (Type I) SS depend on order; the total \(\text{SSR}(x_1,x_2)\) does not.

5.3 Takeaway

\(\text{SSR}(x_1)+\text{SSR}(x_2\mid x_1) = \text{SSR}(x_2)+\text{SSR}(x_1\mid x_2) = \text{SSR}(x_1,x_2)\). For a partial F-test, enter the predictors being tested last.

6.4 Notes

  • anova(fit) with one model shows Type I (sequential) SS.
  • anova(reduced, full) gives the partial F for nested models.
  • aov() is tailored to classical ANOVA; extras include Error(), TukeyHSD(), model.tables().

7 Summary of Tests

7.1 Overall F-test in MLR

Hypotheses: \[\begin{align*} H_0:&\ \beta_1=\cdots=\beta_p=0, \\ H_a:&\ \text{at least one }\beta_j\neq 0. \end{align*}\] Statistic and null distribution: \[ F=\frac{\text{MSR}}{\text{MSE}} =\frac{\text{SSR}/p}{\text{SSE}/(n-p-1)} \ \ \overset{H_0}\sim\ F_{p,\,n-p-1}. \]

7.2 Single coefficient

\[ t=\frac{\hat{\beta}_j}{\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_j)}\sim t_{n-p-1}, \qquad F=t^2\sim F_{1,\,n-p-1}. \]

7.3 Partial F (subset size \(k\))

\[ F=\frac{(\text{SSE}_R-\text{SSE}_F)/k}{\text{SSE}_F/(n-p-1)} \ \ \overset{H_0}\sim\ F_{k,\,n-p-1}. \]

8 Partial \(R^2\)

8.1 Definition (subset given others)

\[ R^2_{\text{partial}} =\frac{\text{SSE}_R-\text{SSE}_F}{\text{SSE}_R} =\frac{\text{SSR}(\text{subset}\mid\text{others})}{\text{SSE}(\text{reduced})}. \]

8.2 Example: mtcars — hp given wt

Code 
SSE_wt    <- sum(residuals(fit_wt)^2)
SSE_wt_hp <- sum(residuals(fit_wt_hp)^2)
R2_hp_given_wt <- (SSE_wt - SSE_wt_hp) / SSE_wt
R2_hp_given_wt
[1] 0.2992009

8.3 Example: mtcars — wt given hp

Code 
fit_hp <- lm(mpg ~ hp, data = mtcars)
SSE_hp <- sum(residuals(fit_hp)^2)
R2_wt_given_hp <- (SSE_hp - SSE_wt_hp) / SSE_hp
R2_wt_given_hp
[1] 0.5643088

8.4 Takeaways

  • Adding predictors cannot decrease overall \(R^2\).
  • Larger partial \(R^2\) corresponds to larger partial F (more evidence against \(H_0\)).
  • Use nested anova() for clear partial tests; Type I SS depend on order.
